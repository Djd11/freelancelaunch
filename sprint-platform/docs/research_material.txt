Phase A: Rapid Skill Acquisition (Days 1–5)
Focus exclusively on the critical shortage skills identified in your demand analysis. 

Day 1: Tool Setup & Environment. Don't teach theory. Teach how to install the exact software stack used in the target jobs (e.g., VS Code extensions, Figma plugins, specific AI models).
Days 2–4: The "Copy-Work" Method. Guide users to replicate 3 existing high-quality projects relevant to the niche. This builds muscle memory faster than passive video watching. For example, if the demand is "Email Automation," have them rebuild 3 specific Klaviyo flows from scratch.
Day 5: The Gap Fill. Identify the specific nuance missing in their replication (e.g., "mobile responsiveness" or "API connection") and provide a targeted micro-lesson to fix it.


Phase B: The "Mock Contract" Simulation (Days 6–10)
This is the differentiator. Most platforms teach skills; yours must teach fulfillment.

Days 6–8: The Capstone Project. Present the user with a realistic brief derived from an actual anonymized job post you found. They must execute this as a paid contract, adhering to the same deadline and constraints.
Days 9–10: Portfolio Packaging. Teach them to document this project as a Case Study, not just a link. The curriculum should force them to write the "Problem," "Solution," and "Result" sections, effectively writing their first proposal draft simultaneously.

Phase C: The "Supply Chain" Integration (Days 11–14)
Connect the learned skill directly to your platform's job feed.

Day 11: Proposal Engineering. Generate a template proposal specifically for the job clusters they just trained for. Since you know the demand, you can pre-write the "I see you need X..." hooks.
Days 12–13: The "First Bid" Challenge. Require them to submit 5 proposals to live jobs that match their new skill profile.
Day 14: Iteration Loop. If they don't get interviews, the curriculum adapts. Your system should analyze why (e.g., price too high? portfolio weak?) and assign a specific 2-hour remedial micro-course. 


3. Technical Implementation for Automation
To scale this without hiring thousands of instructors:

Content Aggregation: Curate existing high-quality open-source resources (documentation, free YouTube tutorials, GitHub repos) and wrap them in your structured daily checklist. You don't need to film new content; you need to sequence existing content effectively.
AI Mentorship: Integrate an LLM trained on the specific job descriptions you scrape. When a user gets stuck, the AI shouldn't just give the answer; it should guide them using the terminology of the job post they are targeting.
Verification: Use automated testing (for code) or peer-review circles (for design/copy) to verify the "Mock Contract" before unlocking the job application phase.

	
	4. Key Differentiator: "Demand-Validated" Badges
Unlike generic certificates, your platform should issue "Demand-Validated" badges.

Display a live counter on the user's profile: "This skill has 450 active jobs right now."
This psychological trigger motivates learners because they see the immediate ROI of the curriculum.
Partner with the hiring side: Allow clients to filter freelancers by "Completed [Specific Demand] Sprint within last 30 days," ensuring the supply is fresh and relevant.
